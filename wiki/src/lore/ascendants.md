Most people never come close to the human limit. A few hundred people alive on the continent have
**Awakened**, broken through it, and most cities can count theirs on two hands. **Ascendants**, who
have gone beyond the limits of the world itself and claimed a Legend, a Domain or a law of their
own, are rarer still: fewer than ten living Ascendants are known by name, and most of them hide it.

The dead ones are another matter. Every child on the continent knows the great **Legends**, and
every city claims at least one.

### The Crownless King

A king of the old kingdoms, about 420 years ago, who sold his own crown to buy a well at a
crossroads, so that five rival caravan houses could meet there without a king over them. He
claimed **Exchange**: anything he traded, he traded fairly, and anything traded fairly in his
presence could not be undone. The Gilded Ascent grew around his well. Its merchants still swear
deals "on the Crownless King", and break them less often than you would expect.

### The Unseen Hand

The greatest thief who ever lived, an Ascendant of **Possession** who could take anything that was
not being held: a purse, a name, a voice, a memory, the balance of a man on a bridge. Nobody knows
what they looked like, which is the point. The Tangle says its master ledger was the Unseen Hand's
own, and that the Mooring is its keeper. The Tangle says a great many things.

### The Voice Beneath Stone

A singer of the deep places who claimed **Echo**. Rillvault's oldest stories say that the first
tunnels were not dug but **sung** open: the Voice sang to the rock until it remembered being
water, and the springs came after. The performance grotto of the Lantern Gardens is shaped like
an open mouth, in their honour.

### The One Who Returned

The exile of the Returning Road who came home to Orath and ended a siege by walking unarmed between
two armies. The faithful say the divine walked with them, or in them. The Spillward's historians
say the exile was an Ascendant of **Return**, who had died in exile and come back, and that no
soldier could raise a weapon against someone already dead. Both sides have been arguing for
centuries, and neither is likely to stop.

### The Last Artificer

The maker of the first Patternwork cores of the Mnemeion in Tessara, who claimed **Pattern**: the
ability to see how anything works, and how it could work better. The Last Artificer taught a
single rule, "build it so that the next person can mend it", and then walked into the Mnemeion's
deepest gallery and did not come out. Some of Tessara's engineers believe the Artificer is still in
there, and that Ison knows it.

### The Beast Without Chains

The first keeper of Keth Veyra to bond with a Brinewing as an equal, rather than a beast to be
harnessed. Their Legend was **Partnership**: no creature bonded to them could be commanded by anyone
else, by any means. The Keepers' Road's pactkeepers follow their teaching, and the binders try very
hard not to mention them.

### The Empty Blade

A duellist of the Second City who claimed **Severance**, and could cut without a sword: a rope, a
contract, a bond between two people, the line between a vampire and the blood it owned. The Empty
Blade founded the [Last Writ](../factions/last-writ.md) after the war that ended in the Sunset
Compact, so that killing in the Eaves would always be answerable to someone.

## The living

> [!WARNING]
> Spoilers for the world's Ascendants below.

Living Ascendants are mostly rumours. A woman in Sky City's Crown is said to have stopped a storm by
refusing to let it pass. An old tortoise on the Keepers' Road is said to be much older than any
tortoise should be. Somebody in the Last Writ has not aged in fifty years. When you start to
[ascend](../play/ascension.md#ascension), the others will notice, and some of them will come to
look at you.
