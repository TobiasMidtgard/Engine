***The Crownless King and the Well*** is a picture book, and the first story nearly everyone in
[the Gilded Ascent](../../cities/gilded-ascent.md) ever hears. It is sold for two chits on the Commons
and given free to every child registered with a Clay Seal, which the Registry thinks is a kindness
and some parents think is the first lesson in owing.

## The story

Five merchants met at a crossroads, each with a cart and a thirst, and found one well. They fought
over it until all five carts were broken and nobody had drunk. A king came by with no soldiers and
a crown on his head, and asked what the matter was. Each merchant told him the well was theirs.

The king took off his crown and asked the first merchant how much water it was worth. The merchant
said a great deal. So the king gave the crown to the well's owner, who was a farmer nobody had
asked, and bought the well, and gave it to all five merchants at once, on one condition: that they
never again let one of them own a thing all of them needed. The merchants agreed, and drank, and
built a city around the well so they could keep an eye on each other.

The king lived in the city for the rest of his life, and anything traded fairly in front of him
could not be undone. When he died, there was no crown to bury.

## What children learn from it

> A fair trade is forever. An unfair one is only until somebody notices.

The Gilded Ascent's merchants still swear deals "on the Crownless King", and break them less often
than you would expect (see [Legends and Ascendants](../ascendants.md#the-crownless-king)). The full,
older story, with the farmer and the old king who refused to come, is in
[The Kings of Ruthven](the-kings-of-ruthven.md).
