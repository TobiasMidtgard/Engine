***The Kings of Ruthven*** is the only history of the kingdom of Ruthven that survived it. It was
written by **Tamar**, an archivist of Saffra, the river city that traded glass with Ruthven before the
sand buried it, and the only complete copy is in the library of
[the Sand-drowned City](../../world/dungeons/the-sand-drowned-city.md). The Chartered Road keeps a
second copy in the Charter House, missing its last chapter, and has never said who took it.

## What it tells you

- Ruthven ruled the central plains, what are now [the Tollands](../../world/regions/the-tollands.md),
  for about three hundred years. It had twelve kings, and buried them in nine barrows on the low
  hills above the Middle River.
- Its crown was gold, and plain, and older than the kingdom: the first king took it from someone
  else, and the history doesn't say who.
- The twelfth king sold the crown to buy a well at a crossroads, so that five rival
  caravan houses could meet there with no king over them. He became
  [the Crownless King](../ascendants.md#the-crownless-king), and the crossroads became
  [the Gilded Ascent](../../cities/gilded-ascent.md).
- His father, the eleventh king, was still alive when he did it, and died a year later. They buried
  him in the Long Barrow with a circlet of iron in place of the crown, because there was no crown
  left (see [the Barrows of Ruthven](../../world/dungeons/barrows-of-ruthven.md)).

## From the book

> The old king would not come to the crossroads. He said a king who sells his crown has sold his
> father's grave with it, and that he would lie in the barrow without one and wait for it to be
> brought back. His son wept, and went to the well, and was a better man than any king of Ruthven
> before him. I do not think the old man ever forgave him. I do not think he has stopped waiting.

## The missing chapter

The Charter House copy stops before the last chapter, which in the Saffra copy is about what the
five caravan houses swore at the well, and what they gave the Crownless King in return. It is the
oldest account of the first seals. Bring the full text to [Ivo Tarrant](../../people/ivo-tarrant.md)
or [Hesper Lorn](../../people/hesper-lorn.md) and each of them will want to know who else has read it.
