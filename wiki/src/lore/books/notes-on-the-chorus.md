***Notes on the Chorus*** is the best book written about [Oruvai](../../cities/oruvai.md) by someone
who isn't part of it. **Isel Thorne**, an engineer of the Pattern Office, went to Oruvai to study how
the chorus-vines carry thought, expecting to find something like Patternwork. She stayed a season,
wore a listener bloom every day, and came home with a book that the Pattern Office published only
after a long argument.

## From the book

> I went expecting a machine. I found a conversation. Every animal in the valley is part of it while
> its paws are on the vines, and none of them is in charge, and nothing I could find was ever
> forgotten.

> A fox stepped off the vines in front of me and was a fox: hungry, curious, rude. An hour later it
> stepped back on and apologised, through the bloom, for the whole species.

> In Tessara a machine must show its pattern. The Chorus has no pattern I could read, and it never
> needs to show it, because there is nobody outside it to show it to. That is what frightened me. I
> think it frightened the Chorus a little that I was frightened.

## Why you might want it

It explains the listener blooms every visitor to Oruvai wears, what the Chorus is likely to think of
you, and the Quiet Verge, where animals rest apart from it. [Moss-Under-Stone](../../people/companions/moss-under-stone.md)
finds it very funny, and asks you to read the part about the fox aloud.
