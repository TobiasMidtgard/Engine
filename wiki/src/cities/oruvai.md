**Oruvai** has no citizens: Oruvai is the citizen. It is one consciousness spread through
millions of animals and plants in a sheltered valley in the west, and it has everything a city
has, from districts and workshops to transport, food and defence, all grown and run through
living cooperation instead of tools.

## Overview

Oruvai does not grow from one giant tree; it fills a broad valley. Braided trunks form towers,
woven canopies become bridges, and beaver-built waterways divide the city into lush terraces.
Different routes suit different bodies: broad moss avenues for herds, raised vineways for
climbers, hollow-root tunnels, high aerial corridors and shallow channels for swimmers and
floating seed bundles. There are no ordinary houses; the city grows spaces for what each creature
needs.

At night, thoughts travel across the valley as waves of coloured leaves, glowing wings and opening
flowers.

## The Chorus

A vast network of conductive **chorus-vines** joins nearly every plant in the valley, and
bioelectric pulses carry simple thoughts through their roots. Animals carry the mind further:

- **Birds** carry complex messages in layered songs.
- **Pollinators** carry chemical signals between isolated gardens.
- **Herds** lend the city long-distance sight, hearing and memory.
- **Burrowers** tend the roots and underground waterways.
- **Fireflies** show the city's mood in coordinated colours.

Animals keep their own personalities and can step out of the Chorus for a while; Oruvai is a mind
formed by constant, willing conversation between distinct lives, not a swarm of drones. It thinks
at different speeds. An otter can report a blocked channel at once; deciding where to grow a new
bridge can take days, and growing it takes years.

Visitors wear a **listener bloom**, a small flowering tendril that translates the Chorus into
feelings, images and scraps of language. It does not open everyone's thoughts to you. Your first
taste of Oruvai might simply be warm soil, thirst and the picture of a blocked stream: the city
asking for help.

## History

Oruvai's oldest memory is a flood. Centuries ago, animals sheltering on the valley's last dry
islands began to sense warnings through the vines beneath their feet. Beavers opened blocked
channels, burrowers uncovered buried roots and birds carried seeds between stranded groves. The
plants that could share their needs survived through the animals that understood them, and that
cooperation slowly became the Chorus. The city remembers its beginning at the **First Rain**, when
animals gather beneath dripping branches and relive fragments of the flood.

## Life in Oruvai

Its harmony is real. No resident deliberately kills another. Plants grow plenty of fruit, nectar
and edible tissue that regrows; even the carnivores eat dense red **marrowfruit**, which gives
them what they need. Herbivores graze in agreed rotations, only where the plants signal that
pruning will help them. Creatures that die naturally are carried into the root gardens and
returned to the soil.

There is no ruler, no ownership and no written law. Decisions emerge from agreement across the
network; disagreements happen, but like competing thoughts in one healthy mind rather than
political factions. Harmony also takes restraint: the Chorus shares what it knows of food stores
and nursery space, so residents plan their breeding and migrations together. Winter stores,
recycled nutrients and the odd import of mineral salts keep the city from feeling effortlessly
abundant.

## Layout and districts

The valley is about three kilometres long. Its districts follow sunlight, water and height.

| District | Where | What is there |
|---|---|---|
| Giving Orchard | South-west entrance | The **Welcome Bower**, where visitors receive listener blooms; branches hung with medicines, fruit, seeds and woven goods. Merchants stay here until invited further in. |
| Sunmeadow Terraces | North-west slopes | Orchards around grazing meadows and irrigation channels, and the **Winter Hollows**, airy stores inside dry root mounds |
| Stillwater Memory | The central lake and its three islands | The **Listening Bowers**, the oldest vines in the city: meeting place, gathering ground and archive. Animals drink here and pass their memories into the Chorus. |
| Weavers' Crown | Tall eastern woods | Workshops spread through linked trees, and the **White Galleries**, where silk is gathered, woven and dried into fabric, rope, gliders and flexible armour |
| Great Nursery | Raised north-east terraces | Spring-fed pools, dry burrows and hanging nests, where every young creature is raised by all |
| Quiet Verge | Glades on the western ridge | Deliberately cut off from the vines. Anyone may rest here for a day or a lifetime, and still have food and company. |
| Returning Gardens | Southern wetlands, downstream | Reed beds, settling pools and decomposition gardens, and **the Last Orchard**, which grows above the resting places of those who died naturally |
| Bloomward | Every approach, thickest in the south | Defensive beds of ivory blooms and rust-red pods |

## Defence

Oruvai has no army. When it is threatened, the plants of the **Bloomward** release a faint,
straw-coloured haze, visible mostly where sunlight catches it. It is not lethal, but it causes
severe discomfort and disorientation and drives intruders back. Residents withdraw before it is
released; they are not immune. The city's protection has limits: strong winds, fire and attacks
from beyond the flower belt are serious dangers.

## Economy

Oruvai exports medicines, living seeds, flexible plant fibres and silk, gathered from abandoned
cocoons or spun willingly. Visitors pay not with coin but by meeting a need the city shows them,
with useful materials or work. Even equipment is a participant: a climbing vine may agree to go
with a traveller who will water it and return it to good soil.

## People

- **Bent-Antler**: an elderly stag who guides visitors and remembers every route the river has
  abandoned.
- **Ashwing**: a jay who bargains through listener blooms and insists that travellers explain any
  object the jay has never seen.
- **The Patient Willow**: a nursery tree who answers slowly, but remembers generations of animals
  sleeping among its roots.

## Quests and rumours

You can borrow the senses of the animals around you through the Chorus, grow living equipment by
helping the valley, and follow routes that change with the city's mood. Sneaking is hard in a city
where nearly every leaf can sense you.

- **A River Borrowed**: in a drought, a settlement upstream has diverted the water. Help both
  communities survive through talk and practical waterworks.
- **The Unfinished Bridge**: a storm destroys a crossing while its living replacement is still
  growing. Restore the way across by climbing, working with the animals and building something
  temporary.
- **The Stolen Summer**: merchants have taken marrowfruit cuttings that cannot survive on their
  own. Get them back and agree a fair trade before the theft becomes a wider conflict.
