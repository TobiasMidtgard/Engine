**Rillvault** is not one great cave but a federation of many tunnel systems, and the world's
greatest producer of food. Mushrooms that glow by themselves light its caverns, and its
mineral-rich water makes plants grow faster and bigger than anywhere above ground.

## Overview

Every tunnel shares the same pale limestone architecture, soft fungal light and abundance of
food. Rillvault's great strength is preservation: it can send nourishing food across enormous
distances without it spoiling, which lets it supply armies, cities and regions struck by famine.

## Law and government

Rillvault is governed by the **Confluence Compact**. Every major tunnel system elects a **Hearth
Delegate**, who speaks for its people, and a **Flow Delegate**, who speaks for its farms, its
water and its ecology. Important laws must pass both, so the crowded market tunnels cannot
overrule the small districts that hold the springs, the nurseries or the water downstream.

Water, springs, mother-fungi and heritage seed strains can never be privately owned. Families and
cooperatives may run their own farms, recipes, businesses and harvests, but the living
foundations of the city belong to everyone.

The people of Rillvault look after one another and their caverns, trade aggressively, welcome
newcomers when they need workers, and stay out of foreign wars, though they are not above using
their food exports as leverage. The great argument of the day is between those who want to export
more and those who fear exhausting the ancient fungal ecosystem.

## Economy

Food is by far the biggest industry:

- Fresh and dried mushrooms; fungal flour, protein paste and cooking oil.
- Giant mineral-fed roots and vegetables; beans, herbs, watercress and cave vines.
- Fish, eels and freshwater shellfish.
- Pickles, sauces, vinegars and preserved meals.
- Medicinal fungal cultures.
- Seeds, spores, fertiliser and farming knowledge.

Rillvault also makes mycelium packing, fungal cloth and ceramic irrigation equipment, but these
come a long way behind food.

## Daily life

Everyday food is surprisingly varied: roasted mineral-root with herb butter, stuffed glowcaps,
mushroom-and-grain loaves, river fish with watercress, thick root and fungus stews, pickled vine
fruit and crisp fried mushroom stems. People drink cold mineral water, root kvass, mushroom ale,
herb infusions and, on special days, a faintly glowing wine made from fungal sugars.

Its people celebrate abundance rather than treasure: huge public feasts, cooking and fermenting
contests, canal and harvest-barge races, prizes for the biggest root or the finest mushroom
strain, mineral baths and water gardens, music in tunnels shaped for sound, glowing lantern
parades and night markets where new foods and recipes change hands.
