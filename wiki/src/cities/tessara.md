**Tessara** is the most technologically advanced city in the world, and from a distance it looks
almost ancient: a warm, terraced city of pale stone, gardens, baths and shaded courtyards built
over a series of river cascades on the fertile south-east coast. Its technology shows only when
you come close: every wall is covered in bands of glazed ceramic tiles, and those mosaics are
the machines.

## Overview

Tessara follows three river terraces down to the sea, with an irregular street plan of twelve
districts. Clean water is drawn in upstream; the factories, freight docks and recycling works sit
downstream, and a whole district lies underground beneath the surface city.

Life here is automated, but never hidden. Driverless trams carry people along the busy routes,
freight runs underground, drones and flying passenger skiffs link the steepest terraces, farms
tend and harvest themselves, and ceramic lamps brighten as people approach. Roof tiles collect
sunlight, and the masonry stores the day's heat for the night. Even the poorest districts have
clean water, heating, clinics and basic automation; wealth shows itself in rare abilities and
restricted patterns, not in who gets sanitation.

## Patternwork

Tessara makes ceramic plates threaded with microscopic channels of engineered Fundaments.
Different plates carry power, heat and water; mechanical instructions; magical signals; stored
information; structural monitoring; and messages between machines. Laid together, the tiles form
physical programs. Replace a handful of tiles and a cargo hoist becomes a construction crane, a
building's climate changes, or a security gate takes its orders from somewhere new. A Tessaran
programmer looks like a master mason carrying a case of tiles.

Its everyday wonders:

- Waste and sewage are separated into reusable materials.
- Underground freight runners supply the shops without filling the streets with carts.
- Public workshops make replacement parts for almost any legal machine.
- Artificial limbs use the same standard joints as tools and work harnesses.
- Walls change their patterns to show leaks, strain and contamination.
- Homes can be rearranged without rebuilding the utilities beneath them.
- Most machines have interchangeable parts and a manual override.
- A modest brooch of personal Patternwork translates, guides, identifies you to a medic and opens
  public services.

The coloured routes in the walls tell you what they carry:

| Colour | Carries |
|---|---|
| Blue | Water |
| Amber | Power and heat |
| Green | Food and living systems |
| Violet | Magic and information |
| Black | Restricted, sealed technology |

## Law and government

Tessara's defining law: **any machine with power over a person must reveal its pattern.**
Security systems, medical devices, public works and the machinery of government must all be open
to inspection. Tessarans believe that technology nobody can understand is simply another form of
tyranny.

The city is run by **Ison**, a governing machine mind that serves as chief executive. It sets the
public budgets, plans construction, coordinates food production and manages emergencies. The
citizens write its governing charter and can challenge its decisions through the Open Measure.
Ison's data centre, **the Mnemeion**, stands in the civic complex: public galleries look down on
ceramic computing arrays, and terminals let anyone examine a decision or submit a proposal. Backup
cores in each district keep essential services running, and the heat of the computing arrays warms
the baths and nurseries.

## Factions and guilds

- **The Open Measure**: civic engineers, elected auditors and champions of technology anyone can
  read.
- **The Closed Kilns**: rival invention houses that guard their patterns and fund dangerous
  research. Their black-glazed parts are smaller and more powerful, and no outsider can inspect or
  repair them.
- **The Certifiers**: decide which inventions may connect to the public network, which makes them
  the quietest power in the city.
- **The Menders**: the maintenance union, with access to almost every wall, tunnel and utility
  chamber in Tessara.
- **The Hairline**: smugglers, thieves and outlaw artificers who make counterfeit adapters and
  hide changes inside legitimate mosaics.
- **Ferris's Tempered Hand**: priests of the Maker, who teach that building something without
  teaching others to repair it is sacrilege.
- **The Aegis**: the city guard.
- **[The Pattern Office](../factions/pattern-office.md)**: the engineers who keep the city's
  Patternwork running.

The Closed Kilns innovate faster; the Open Measure protects the city from monopolies, hidden
surveillance and catastrophic faults. Neither side is entirely right.

## Arms and armour

The Aegis wear tunics, cloaks and discreet ceramic fittings. In a fight, their equipment projects
fitted hoplite armour, round shields and energy weapons. Every set runs on replaceable ceramic
power tiles and works without Ison. Protection, movement and weapons share one energy reserve, so
pushing one leaves less for the others.

## Getting around

Trams run the busy passenger routes, underground runners carry bulk freight, and drones and
passenger skiffs link the terraces that are hard to reach. Lighting works with transport:
pavement lights guide people as they approach, and in an emergency the escape routes light up as
the trams redirect the crowds.

## Quests and rumours

Hacking in Tessara is physical: a thief can slip into a maintenance gallery and swap three tiles
to divert a shipment; an artificer can build new pattern chains; a mage can trace or corrupt the
signals running through a wall. Cutting one route might open a vault, but it might also shut down
a clinic nearby or flood a courtyard of homes.

> [!WARNING]
> Spoilers for Tessara's main quest below.

**The Black Pattern.** Tessara's public network is nearly full. One of the Closed Kilns has made
black-glazed plates powerful enough to modernise the whole city, and offers Ison a far more
capable computing core whose workings are sealed, but only that house can make, inspect or repair
them. You can protect the open system and accept slower progress, back the Closed Kiln, steal and
publish the pattern, help the Menders build an imperfect open copy, or split the city into two
networks that cannot talk to each other. Whatever you choose changes how the city looks, as
colourful public mosaics, black sealed bands or improvised patchwork spread across its walls.
