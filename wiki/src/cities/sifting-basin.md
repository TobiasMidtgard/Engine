The **Sifting Basin** is a desert city that grows its wealth out of the sand. Wind carries
mineral-rich sand into its basin from a crumbling mountain range, small burrowing animals called
**latticebacks** sift the minerals out and build them into their nests, and the city harvests the
empty nests for its furnaces.

## Overview

The city fills a desert hollow where the prevailing winds drop sand worn from the mountains. Its
dry central terraces follow the basin's curves, and neighbourhoods climb the slopes around them in
irregular streets, courtyards and bridges. Turquoise water is kept to cisterns, gardens, fountains
and civic canals, so blue-tiled pools stand out against the terracotta walls.

## History

The city's founders noticed that the brittle nests scattered across the desert made unusually rich
furnace slag. Generations of experiments turned the gathering of old nests into a whole craft of
animal keeping.

## Latticebacks

Latticebacks are small, social, armoured burrowers. They sift sand to eat wind-blown seeds, fungal
spores and the thin living crust that wakes after brief rains; their keepers add fermented seed
husks and scraps. The creatures in their gut separate mineral grains from the grit they swallow.
They spit out most of the quartz and mix the concentrated minerals with a binder they secrete to
build porous honeycomb nurseries that shield their young from predators and from heat and cold.

Latticebacks only concentrate what is already there: they cannot make copper from sand that holds
none, so every collection ground yields something different, and even a rich nest must be refined
before it becomes metal. Once the young leave, the colony moves to fresh ground. Keepers keep some
empty nests as shelters and harvest the rest. Overcrowding, the wrong feed or harvesting too early
can ruin the next breeding season.

Windcatcher walls funnel the sand into the dry sifting grounds, and the cleaned sand left over goes
to the glassworks.

## Law and government

The Sifting Basin is a chartered city, governed by representatives of its residential wards, its
colony keepers and its trading houses. The public wells and the harvesting basin belong to the
city; households and businesses hold operating rights that must be renewed.

Every resident is guaranteed a household share of water. Workshops, baths, gardens and private
pools buy more. Its weakness is debt: merchants advance money for food, tools and colony care, then
claim a share of the harvest to come, so a keeper can legally hold a plot and still have almost no
say over what it earns.

## Daily life

Heavy work starts before sunrise and begins again toward evening; in the hottest hours, business
moves under the arcades and into cool rooms. People eat flatbread, lentils, preserved vegetables,
dates and goat's cheese, and the mineral trade pays for most of the city's grain and furnace fuel.

Every year **the Opening of the Combs** begins when keepers release the first empty nurseries for
harvest. Families show off delicate fragments of empty nest, glassmakers unveil new work, and
contests test weighing, animal handling and sail setting.
