**The Second City** is two cities stacked on top of each other. At street level it is a lawful,
prosperous port of boulevards, courts and lamplit arcades. Above it, across the rooftops and the
upper floors, runs **the Eaves**: one continuous rooftop street, from the harbour to the city
walls, where vampires rule a criminal metropolis of black magic, werebeasts, necromancy and murder
for hire.

The people below gave the upper city its name long ago, the way you might speak of a second
family, and the name stuck to the whole place.

## Overview

Both cities need each other, and both pretend otherwise. The Boulevard's merchants buy the Eaves'
poisons, curses and silence; the Eaves buy the Boulevard's food, cloth, servants and blood. Every
building is split: shops and homes on the lower floors, and above a certain height, doors that
open only from the inside, windows painted black and stairs that nobody admits are there.

The line between them is **the third floor**. Above it, the Boulevard's law no longer reaches.

| Look | White stone, green copper, gas-bright arcades | Wet black slate, tarnished brass, oxblood glass and cold blue ward-light |

## The Sunset Compact

Generations ago the two cities fought a quiet war that nobody won. It ended in **the Sunset
Compact**, which still keeps the peace:

- **The Eaves never feed in the Boulevard.** No vampire may take blood at street level, and no
  werebeast may change below the roofs.
- **The Magistracy never climbs above the third floor.** Its watchmen don't follow anyone up, and
  its courts don't hear crimes committed there.
- **Each side returns the other's fugitives**, for a fee.

Everybody knows the Compact is cracking. Bodies have been found on the Boulevard, drained, and
the Magistracy has begun to hire people who can climb.

## The law of the Eaves

The Eaves are not lawless. They are a place where the forbidden is licensed, taxed and protected:

- **Killing needs a Red Writ.** Anyone may buy a writ naming a victim, at a price set by who the
  victim is. A killing without one is punished as theft: of the victim's blood, flesh and memories,
  which all belonged to someone.
- **Blood is property.** Vampires feed only on blood they own, buy or win by contract. Most of it
  comes from the **blood houses**, where people sell it willingly for good money.
- **Turning someone needs an inheritance agreement**, signed and witnessed, for vampirism and
  lycanthropy alike. An unagreed bite is the gravest crime in the Eaves.
- **Every corpse is registered.** Bodies, memories and souls have separate owners, and the
  necromancers of the Pale Archive keep the books.

The assassins of [the Last Writ](../factions/last-writ.md) verify and register every Red Writ.
Their name comes from it: the last writ is the one that has been checked and cannot be refused.

## The Night Council

Five seats rule the Eaves, and none of them trusts the others:

| Seat | Held by | Controls |
|---|---|---|
| **The Old Blood** | The eldest vampire, never seen by day | The blood houses and the blood reserves |
| **The Pack-Speaker** | The chief of the werebeast clans | The Moon Runs and every route across the roofs |
| **The Registrar of the Dead** | A necromancer of the Pale Archive | Corpses, the dead workforce and the testimony of the dead |
| **The Warden of Wards** | A black mage | The wards that keep sunlight, fire and the Magistracy out |
| **The Broker** | The only mortal seat | Trade with the Boulevard and the outside world, and blackmail |

## Getting around

The Eaves are one long street, but not a flat one. Bridges and gantries cross the boulevards,
walkways run along the gutters, and the rooftops rise and fall over chimneys, domes and towers. A
good climber can cross the city without touching the ground; a bad one meets the Magistracy's
watch on the cobbles. Night-sails, vast canvas awnings, shade the roof street by day, so vampires
can move under them when the sun is up, as long as the wind behaves.

Ways up are guarded: stairwells behind black doors, laundry lifts, chimney ladders and the
servants' stairs of the rich. Climbing up uninvited is legal. Coming down again alive is the hard
part.

## Economy

The Boulevard is a busy port: fish, wine, wool, books and shipbuilding. The Eaves sell what nobody
admits to buying: poisons, curses, forged identities, testimony from the dead, contracts on lives,
and safe passage for people who need to disappear. Blood is the Eaves' real currency, and good
blood houses pay their donors more than a dockworker earns in a week.

## Quests and rumours

- **[A Writ in Question](../quests/a-writ-in-question.md)**: the Last Writ's first job for you.
  Escort a witness to a hearing that will decide whether a contract is real.
- **Blood on the Boulevard**: three drained bodies at street level. Either someone in the Eaves has
  broken the Compact, or someone wants it to look that way.
- **The unagreed bite**: a young clerk of the Courts has been turned without an agreement, and both
  cities want him.
- **The silver merchant**: a Boulevard smith sells silvered blades, which are banned in the Eaves.
  The Night Council wants his shop burned; the Magistracy wants him protected.
- **Testimony**: a murdered magistrate's memories are for sale in the Pale Archive.
