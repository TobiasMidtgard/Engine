**Orath** is the spiritual centre of the known world. Four religions began here, each remembering
a different miracle at the same spring, and every great faith claims the city, yet none is strong
enough to own it. Pilgrims arrive expecting certainty; the people who live here know which temple
serves breakfast without asking what you believe.

## Overview

Orath lies in a dry mountain basin around **the First Spring**, an ancient source of water that
has never stopped flowing, through sieges, droughts and magical catastrophes. From a distance the
city is beautiful and overwhelmingly holy: a great white crown rising above tightly packed ochre
districts, four streams of water cutting through the streets and endless columns of pilgrims
walking toward its dark, open centre. Once inside, it is crowded and intensely political.

The city is built of pale limestone, black foundation stone and weathered bronze. Each religious
quarter adds its own colours and ornament; the Hollow Crown stays deliberately neutral.

## The Hollow Crown

Orath's great temple is a colossal ring built around the First Spring:

- **Seven terraces** rise into an artificial mountain almost 500 metres across, open to the sky in
  the middle.
- **Forty immense stone ribs** ring the opening, so from across the basin the temple looks like a
  crown.
- **The innermost sanctuary** lies deep below the street, so pilgrims climb the outside before
  descending toward the water. Its deepest chamber is deliberately small: even an emperor must
  enter without a grand following.
- **Four bridges** meet over the open centre, letting people cross above the water without
  standing on its source.
- **Four great waterways** carry the spring's overflow down the temple and through the city.
- At night tens of thousands of lamps light its arches, while the open centre stays dark.

Its black foundations belong to a civilisation older than any of the four faiths, so each faith
believes it is the true heir of the temple's first builders. Pilgrims perform **the Crossing**:
they enter by one religion's gate, climb the terraces, cross above the sacred well and leave by
another gate. Each faith prescribes its own route. Weapons must be sealed before entering.

## The four faiths

| Faith | How it began | Belief and worship | In everyday life |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Solitary Light | Its prophet received a revelation when the first sunrise after a generation-long war struck the spring | One divine presence lights every person. The faithful face the eastern sunrise arch. | Schools, public hearings and oath courts |
| The Deep Choir | The oldest faith: people heard voices rising from the caverns beneath the spring | The dead remain part of the community. The faithful recite names in the echoing chambers below the spring. | Burial, family histories, inheritance records and care of the bereaved |
| The Returning Road | An exile came home and ended a siege by walking unarmed between the two armies | The divine once came to Orath as a stranger nobody recognised. Hospitality and reconciliation honour that return. | Pilgrim hostels, interpreters, caravan escorts and sanctuary houses |
| The Covenant of Ash | The city burned, but the spring kept flowing beneath the ruins | Surviving catastrophe creates a duty to others. Worship means tending wounds, cooking meals and rebuilding homes. | Hospitals, communal kitchens, firefighting and rebuilding |

None of the faiths is united. A compassionate priest and a ruthless administrator can share the
same doctrine and each sincerely believe the other is endangering it:

- The Solitary Light argues over whether rulers must confess in public.
- Some custodians of the Deep Choir lavish care on rich ancestral houses; others insist an unnamed
  beggar deserves the same remembrance.
- The Returning Road's open door protects refugees, but also shelters people running from justice.
- Some of the Covenant of Ash accept any donation that pays for relief; others refuse money made
  through exploitation.

Many smaller religions keep shrines in Orath too. Some hold that all four revelations are true;
others believe the city is a dangerous spiritual deception.

## History

Orath began as a settlement around the spring, where water rose from a rocky outcrop in a dry
basin. Travellers left offerings beside it long before anyone built a temple. The oldest surviving
inscriptions record names, shares of water and promises to returning travellers; whether these
were practical agreements or the first prayers is still fiercely disputed. Long ago **the Great
Burning** left the western gate blackened, and it has never been repaired.

The event that shaped modern Orath happened **168 years ago**. In the last holy war, the religious
commanders sealed their precincts while civilians crowded the streets between them. Hospital
workers, gravediggers and hostel keepers from all four traditions opened the service passages into
the Hollow Crown against orders, and thousands survived beneath the sanctuary. After the war every
faith claimed the rescuers as proof of its own teachings. The rescuers' own testimony was less
convenient: they had acted because people were dying outside.

## Law and government

The **Accord of Four Keys** gave each faith a key to the innermost sanctuary; all four are needed
to open its ceremonial doors. The waterworks can always be reached separately, so no religious
quarrel can cut off the city's water.

The elected **Fifth Seat** governs civil life: water, building permits, crowds and foreign access,
which makes this supposedly ordinary office one of the most corruptible in the world. The
**Wardens of the Threshold** enforce the accord and protect the processions; they may follow a
religion but cannot hold religious office or take foreign military pay. No foreign army may enter
Orath, and conquering it would make the invader the enemy of several religions at once.

## Layout and districts

Seen from above, Orath is a wheel. The Hollow Crown is the hub; four processional avenues run out
from its gates, each through one religion's main district; a shared inner ring holds archives,
religious courts, monasteries and embassies; and the outer city is pilgrim hostels, baths, markets,
animal yards and camps.

| District | Where | What is there |
|---|---|---|
| The Dawn Steps | East of the Crown | The Solitary Light's teaching courtyards, bronze-screened prayer halls and public courts. Rich families pay for beautiful stairways here, making sure their names are where the pilgrims will see them. |
| The Underchoir | Along the northern rock | The Deep Choir's quarter, linking homes on the terraces to burial galleries and chambers of remembrance. Professional reciters keep family histories; some spend their evenings collecting the names of people who died with no one to claim them. |
| The Returning Yards | By the southern gates | Huge courtyard hostels, stables, translators' booths and baggage stores. In festivals their covered galleries become dormitories. |
| The Ash Gardens | West of the temple | Hospitals, recovery courtyards and communal ovens around the unrepaired gate of the Great Burning. Patients leave small handmade things in its cracks. |
| The Fifth Quarter | Between the precincts | Mixed-faith families, foreigners, sceptics and tradespeople above shops and shared courtyards. A household may keep several faiths' holy days, and quietly ignore the rules that make them clash. |
| Old Orath | Beneath everything | Buried streets, forgotten sanctuaries and the machinery that carries the sacred water |

The spring lies below the temple terraces but above most of the city, so channels carry its water
downhill through the foundations to the fountains. Rain cisterns add to the supply. The engineers
who keep it all running are a powerful community whose work crosses every religious boundary.

## Economy

Pilgrims keep Orath alive, along with medicine, manuscript copying, bronze lamp-making, ceremonial
cloth and stone restoration. The terraces around the city grow fruit, pulses and some grain, but
Orath depends on imported food, and its granaries matter as much as its relic vaults. Merchants
profit from pilgrims' lodgings, ceremonial goods and festival shortages; thieves trade in forged
relics and illegally bottled spring water.

## Daily life

People love courtyard music, storytelling contests, rooftop suppers and arguing over which bakery
makes the proper festival bread. Once a year, on **the Unbarred Night**, the city remembers the
wartime rescue: a lamp outside a home offers any stranger food and shelter. Nobody has to light
one, but turning a guest away after lighting it is a serious disgrace. Some households save all
year to host in style; others offer one bowl of soup and a place by the stove.

## Quests and rumours

- **[A Miracle Shared](../quests/a-miracle-shared.md)**: the Accord of Four Keys' first job for you.
  Water has begun to rise upward in a shared chamber. Four faiths claim the miracle, and the
  Spillward says it is a spill.

You can earn a separate reputation with each faith, and work as a guide, relic hunter, temple
guard, investigator of the faiths or go-between. Festival crowds change the routes and the
security of the whole city.

> [!WARNING]
> Spoilers for Orath's main quest below.

You arrive just before the Unbarred Night, as the First Spring begins to weaken. The water
engineer **[Ilyan Dareth](../people/ilyan-dareth.md)** has found an abandoned channel beneath the Underchoir, but later
generations built burial galleries inside its old reservoir. **[Sister Avara](../people/companions/sister-avara.md)**, a physician of the
Covenant of Ash, wants to prepare for shortages at once; several officials fear that announcing it
will start hoarding before the pilgrims arrive. Beyond the blocked channel lies **the fifth
passage**, an archive older than all four religions, recording how earlier communities kept the
spring alive together. It cannot say whether anyone met a god here, but it can show who has
altered the waterworks, hidden failures or claimed ownership they never had.

Your first job is small: escort a maintenance crew through a disputed burial gallery. By the time
you come back, a custodian has withdrawn permission, a worker has vanished, and someone has begun
selling miraculous water from a spring the public still believes will never run dry.
