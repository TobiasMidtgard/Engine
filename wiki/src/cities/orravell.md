**Orravell** is a city built in and around a single ancient tree, **Avarren**, whose trunk is wider
than a fortress and whose canopy spreads several kilometres across. It looks peaceful: ivory-grey
bark, amber windows, turquoise awnings, magnificent gardens and prosperous families. Its beauty is
the reward of conquest, paid for by mercenary service, plunder and the weapons it makes.

A soldier comes home to a beautiful family balcony. The children get foreign sweets, the spouse
gets jewellery, and the household celebrates another successful campaign. Somewhere else, a family
is searching for the belongings, and the people, that the expedition took.

## Overview

Homes, markets, farms, workshops, temples and military posts all hang from the same living tree.
A vast spiral street winds around and through the trunk; gates hidden inside its decorative arches
can divide it into dozens of sealed sections. Many homes keep reinforced shutters, protected
stairs, weapon lockers and rooms meant for defence. Visitors are less surprised by the soldiers,
who are proudly everywhere, than by how completely the pleasant architecture turns into a fortress,
and how much force keeps the comfort inside it going.

What a newcomer notices first: a porter stepping aside for a family's prestigious clasp; a foreign
specialist refused permission to leave; a veteran pointing out which campaign paid for their
balcony; a visitor recognising their own family's tapestry hanging in someone else's home.

## History

Orravell's first people survived by raiding, then found that neighbouring rulers would pay well for
their military skill. Successful commanders became rich patrons, households invested in
expeditions and workshops grew into factories. Over generations, victory became the normal way to
get ahead: a good campaign means better homes for soldiers' families, orders for the factories,
pensions for veterans, commissions for young officers and cheap luxuries from abroad, while
captured mines and tolls pay out between wars.

Then a long regional peace brought unemployment, unpaid military debts and collapsing investment.
The present ruler, **Cael Ordan**, rose out of that crisis, promising national renewal through
discipline, permanent mobilisation and expansion.

## Law and government

Orravell is a one-party dictatorship under **the Crown Ascendant**. Cael Ordan keeps the
deceptively gentle title of **First Gardener**, as well as **Commander of the Crown**; his portraits
show him inspecting factories, greeting soldiers and talking to children. **The Council of
Stewards** carries out his decisions through appointed officials, and **the Office of Civic
Integrity** runs political investigations, censorship, informants and the punishment of anyone
accused of disloyalty. Independent unions and opposition parties are banned.

The state's creed is **the Doctrine of the Greater Canopy**: Orravell has outgrown its borders,
and the lands around it will only know lasting order under its rule. Court maps draw a great
symbolic canopy over every territory the government wants next, and its edges move with the
government's ambitions. Outsiders are called "the unrooted", and opponents are accused of letting
"rot" into the nation.

Two factions compete inside the regime. Both believe in Orravell's supremacy; they argue over how
best to profit from it:

- **The Long Shade** wants annexation and a growing territorial empire.
- **The Still Bough** prefers profitable mercenary contracts, raids and selective deals, without
  costly occupations.

## Society

| Group | Place in society |
|---|---|
| Boughborn | Families registered as descendants of Avarren's founders: the best housing, schooling, protected rations, military commissions and public benefits |
| Licensed residents | Useful merchants, specialists and workers, whose right to stay depends on permits and sponsors |
| Bound dependants | Captives and people trapped in forced-labour settlements, with restricted movement and work |
| Foreign tributaries | Communities allowed some self-government in return for money, materials, soldiers or labour |

Boughborn status comes only from the state's household register. A foreign worker's child can be
born inside the tree, speak the same language and live on the same streets for decades without
it. Powerful sponsors can win an exceptional outsider honorary status. In truth the founders were a
mixed people, and their records are incomplete.

## Layout and districts

| Level | In peace | In war |
|---|---|---|
| The Rootcourt | The main entrance, guesthouses, the public market and the Prize Exchange | A walled basin ringed by firing positions |
| The Lower Hearth | Workshops, kitchens and storehouses inside the trunk | Armouries, reserve barracks and sealed gates |
| The Great Boughs | Crowded homes on the branches, full of furniture and banners from past campaigns | Routes for the infantry and fortified chokepoints |
| The Garden Crown | Orchards, livestock terraces, hanging farms, officers' clubs and victory banquets | Clear lines of fire disguised as farmland |
| The Sunward Canopy | Temples, schools and gardens | Elite archers, scouts, command posts, living artillery and Hushwing Station |
| The Heartwood | The sacred chamber around the tree's living core, with the council and the archives | The final command centre and last line of defence |

Avarren's roots draw and filter groundwater and pump it through hidden pipes, so ordinary taps
supply homes, kitchens and workshops throughout the tree.

## Economy

Avarren can house a civilisation, but not feed or supply one: Orravell needs grain, metal, fuel and
materials from outside. Heavy ore work happens in the territories it controls; the tree itself does
skilled manufacturing and final assembly.

| Works | Where | What it makes |
|---|---|---|
| Hearthline Arsenal | Halls in the lower trunk | Standard weapon parts and armour fittings; assembly, inspection and repair |
| Crown Looms | Broad lower branches | Uniforms, tents, harness webbing, sailcloth and medical cloth |
| Root Foundries | Stone halls between the root buttresses, well clear of the living wood | Casting, forging and melting down captured metal |
| Resin Houses | Ventilated workshops on the outside | Glues, coatings, composite panels and protective cases |
| Wingworks | Galleries below Hushwing Station | Building and repairing gliders |
| March Stores | Protected inner chambers | Preserved food, medical supplies and expedition kit |
| Prize Exchange | The freight halls of the Rootcourt | Valuing, selling and sharing out captured goods |

At the Prize Exchange, plunder becomes respectable trade: valued silver, foreign paintings,
military equipment, confiscated stock and the rights to future harvests. A captured master
craftsperson might be given a comfortable room and still be forbidden to leave.

## The army

- **Crown Companies**: archers and marksmen on hidden platforms and balconies, with longbows, compact
  mechanical launchers and throwing spears. Officers direct their fire by numbered branches and
  coloured flowering trees.
- **Hushwalkers**: scouts and ambushers who move along the undersides of branches in moss cloaks and
  soft hooked boots, picking off messengers, burning supplies and cutting off officers.
- **Bough Cohorts**: spear-and-shield infantry inside the trunk, armoured in dark resin plates over
  woven bark.
- **Graftwrights**: gardener-engineers who grow thorn walls, rotating bridges, vine screens, hidden
  passages and living ballista branches.

Their colours are charcoal resin, dull bronze and very dark green.

**Hushwing Station** is a hidden glider hangar beneath a great limb of the canopy, screened by leaves
and weather cloth, with folding gliders, harness racks, repair benches and launch doors facing
different winds. Gliders are limited by weather, load, height and skill, and many missions end far
below and walk home. Its crews say: "The mission ends when the last harness is hung."
