Almost everything you can buy, you can make. Crafting happens at **stations**: an alchemy bench, a
forge, a tinker's table, a kitchen and an enchanting circle. Every city has public stations you can
rent by the hour, the guilds open theirs to members, and you can build your own into a
[home](../play/homes.md). Each craft is a skill that grows with use and with training (see
[Skills](../play/skills.md#craft-skills)), and what you make is only as good as your rank, your
materials and your station.

## Quality

Everything you make comes out **Common**, **Fine** or **Masterwork** (see
[Items](../items/index.md#rarity)). Three things decide which:

- **Your rank.** A Novice makes Common things. Fine needs Apprentice or better; Masterwork needs
  Master, or a perk that lets you reach past your rank.
- **Your station.** A field kit or a campfire can't make anything Fine. A public station can't make
  anything Masterwork; a guild's or a master's can.
- **The work itself.** Every craft has a moment that rewards attention: the heat window of a brew,
  the rhythm at the anvil, the last tile in a chain. Get it exactly right and the piece comes out
  one quality better than your rank allows, once in a while.

Things you make are yours: they carry **your mark**, and anyone who knows it knows who made them. A
member of the [Makers' Compact](../factions/makers-compact.md) adds the lodge's mark, which raises
the price by a third.

## Selling what you make

Smiths buy weapons and armour, alchemists buy potions and ingredients, and general traders buy
anything cheaply (see [Trading](../play/trading.md#merchants)). The lodges post **contracts** in every
hall: a hundred arrowheads for the Chartered Road's outriders, a dozen healing potions for a
caravan, a lock for a widow who has been robbed twice. A contract pays a fifth more than a shop, and
counts towards your rank in the guild.
