**Relics** are objects with names. Some are famous because of what they do, some because of who
owned them, and some only because people keep trying to steal them. A Storied item is almost
impossible to sell, because everyone who could afford it knows exactly whose it is. A **Divine**
item is something else again: touched by a god, or made to wound one, and the only kind of thing
that can.

### The Crownless King's Balance

A brass balance small enough to carry, older than the Gilded Ascent. The Crownless King is said to
have weighed his crown on it against the deeds to the Middle Well. Any bargain weighed on it is
fair: if one side cheats, the pans will not settle. The Chartered Road uses it once a year, to
weigh the new Glass Seal holders' oaths. Stealing it would make every deal in the city suspect.

### The Empty Blade's hilt

A plain steel hilt with no blade. The Last Word carries it at every hearing of the Last Writ. In
the hand of anyone else it is a hilt; in the hand of the Empty Blade, it cut anything. Some members
believe that whoever next claims **Severance** will find it cuts for them, too.

### The unbuckled harness

A Brinewing harness of old leather and brass, hung in the Old Harness House among the retired
harnesses that keep the stories of generations. The Beast Without Chains made it for their partner,
who never let them buckle it. It hangs open, as a promise. The binders of the Keepers' Road would
very much like to know what it does when it is closed.

### Oathkeeper

A two-handed sword of folded steel, made in the Mint's own forges for the first warden-captain of the
Registry a hundred years ago. Every captain since has had their oath engraved on the blade, one under
the other; Garrick's is the eleventh, and there is room for one more. He won't give it up, and if he
dies with it in his hand it goes back to the Registry, which will give it to whoever replaces him. If
he leaves the Registry's service, he leaves it hanging in the Wardens' hall on the Ledger Floor, and you can take it.

### Patience

Master Aldous Pike's masterpiece, eleven years in the making: a plain, narrow sword that cannot be
dulled. Pike will not sell it. He will lend it, once, to someone he trusts with a job he thinks only it
can do, and he will want it back. The Tangle has a standing offer of 40 ascents for it, which Pike
finds very funny.

### The Toll-Taker

The great axe of the last commander of the Old Toll Fort, who collected the Chartered Road's tolls by
force in the years before the charter, and passed to every brigand who held the fort after him. Marrok
Teague carries it now. It is said to take a toll of everyone it touches, and anyone who has fought
him will tell you it does.

### The Keeper's Pen

A plain steel pen with a nib of black stone, the same stone as the doors. Quell found it beside the
Sixth Register and has used no other since. In your hand it can write one thing into the Register,
once, and have it stay written: a well opened for good, a name nobody can strike out, a door that
will never open again. What you write is the last thing the pen does. Some endings depend on it
(see [The Great Restriking](../quests/main/the-great-restriking.md)).

## Divine items

> [!WARNING]
> Spoilers for the Godslayer path below.

Only something touched by a god can wound one. Divine items are found, earned or made at a terrible
price, and every one of them is wanted by someone who has a god in mind.

| Relic | Where it is | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **The Sixth Seal** | Around the Sealkeeper's neck, the Gilded Ascent | A seal no house struck: the key to the temple of the God of Locked Doors |
| **The tine** | With the Last Word, the Second City | A tine from the antlers of the Antlered Hunt, taken by a hunter who did not run |
| **Ferris's hand** | The Tempered Hand's shrine, Tessara | The hand Ferris drew from the Maker's forge, still warm |
| **Spring-water of the first dawn** | Nowhere yet | Water drawn from the First Spring at the moment it fails, if it fails |

### The Sixth Seal

A disc of black stone with no number, as old as the Counting Deep. Sealkeepers pass it down without
knowing what it opens, and swear never to use it. It opens the first door of the
[Temple of Locked Doors](../quests/the-temple-of-locked-doors.md), and it is the only key the god
ever made.

### The tine

A curved tine of bone as long as a forearm, which never stops feeling warm and alive in the hand.
The Last Writ's records say a hunter of the Empty Blade's time faced the Antlered Hunt instead of
running, and broke it off. Set into a blade or an arrowhead, it can wound a god.

### Ferris's hand

The steel hand Ferris drew from the Maker's forge, kept by the priests who carry Ferris's name. It
is the holiest object in Tessara. It is also, as the priests know and do not say, a piece of a god's
own work, and so it can wound a god, including the one who made it. Melting it down would make the
most powerful weapon in the world, and destroy the Maker's most loved relic.

## Making a divine weapon

A Divine material in a Masterwork weapon makes a weapon that can wound a god. It takes a master
smith or enchanter, the relic itself, and something more that depends on the god you mean to face:
a true name, a broken law, a promise. That is part of becoming a
[Godslayer](../play/progression.md#godslayer).
