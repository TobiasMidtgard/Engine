**The Grey Coast** is the cold, foggy shore around [the Second City](../../cities/second-city.md), where the
Crown Road reaches the sea. It is a coast of black cliffs and grey harbours, of fishing towns that
shutter their windows at sunset and moors where the werebeast clans of the Eaves run on the nights
the city is too small for them. Under the Sunset Compact, the Eaves don't feed on the Boulevard.
They never promised anything about the coast.

## Geography

The cliffs run north and south of the Second City for three days in each direction, broken by
harbours where rivers come down off the moors. Inland, the moors are heather, peat, standing water
and fog. Offshore, sea stacks rise out of the water, and the tallest, **the Weeper**, has a tower on
top that drips salt water down its sides in every weather.

## Settlements

| Place | What is there |
|---|---|
| **Fogwick** | A fishing town of grey stone with a lighthouse, a smokehouse and an inn (the Lamp and Net, 1 mark) that shutters at dusk |
| **Cape Sorrow** | A headland chapel where widows of the fishing fleet leave lanterns. The keeper sells silvered knives. |
| **Saltgrave** | A smugglers' harbour that trades with the Eaves by night, and with the Boulevard by day |
| **The Moon Runs' edge** | Where the roofs of the Eaves end and the werebeast clans' hunting moors begin |

## Points of interest

| Place | What is there |
|---|---|
| **The Weeper** | A sea stack with a tower on it that weeps salt water |
| **The Wreckers' Steps** | A cliff stair down to a beach full of wrecks, some of them recent |
| **Blackpeat Moor** | The clans' moor, where the Pack-Speaker settles disputes at the full moon |

## Rumours and work

- Fogwick's lighthouse keeper has stopped lighting the lamp. Ships have started coming in anyway.
- A child of Saltgrave was bitten on the moor, and the town wants to drown her before the moon is full.
- The tower on the Weeper has a light in it for the first time in thirty years.
- A wreck on the Steps carries Registry seals in its hold: three hundred of them, blank.
