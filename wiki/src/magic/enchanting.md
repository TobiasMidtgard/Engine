**Enchanting** binds a spell into an object so that it works without you casting it. It is a
profession like smithing or alchemy: a smith who never casts can still enchant a little, and a mage
who studies the [Artifice branch](shaping.md#artifice) of Shaping can enchant far more. The best
enchanters are both.

## How an enchantment works

An enchantment is a **spell you have crafted** (see [Spellcrafting](spellcrafting.md)), bound into
a **vessel** (the item), with a **trigger** that decides when it fires. At an enchanting bench you
choose all three, and the bench shows you how much of the vessel's capacity the spell uses, how
much charge it needs and what your accent will look like.

| Item | Trigger | Works like |
|---|---|---|
| **Weapon** | On hit | The spell lands on whatever you strike (the Imbue cast type) |
| **Armour and shields** | On block, or when struck | The spell fires at whoever hit you, or on you |
| **Clothing and jewellery** | Worn | The spell stays on you while you wear it (the Self or Aura cast type) |
| **Tools** | On use | The spell fires when you use the tool |
| **Scrolls** | Read once | Anyone who can read can cast it, once |
| **Wands** | Charges | Cast it again and again until the charges run out |
| **Runestones** | Placed | The spell becomes a rune on a surface, firing when triggered |
| **Turrets** | Set down | The spell is cast by a small construct, on its own |

## Vessels

Every material can hold only so much magic. A spell bigger than its vessel's **capacity** will not
bind, or binds badly and fails.

| Vessel | Capacity | Holds best |
|---|---|---|
| Wood | 1 | Anything small |
| Iron | 2 | Anything |
| Steel | 3 | Anything |
| Silver | 4 | Clean magic, and spells against the undead |
| Gold | 5 | Mind |
| Patternwork tile | 6 | Anything, but every pattern can be read |
| Brinewing scale | 6 | Fire and Frost |
| Bone | 6 | Dark magic only |
| Avarren heartwood | 8 | Life |
| Stormglass | 10 | Storm only |
| Basin glass | 12 | Anything |
| Hushstone | None | It drinks enchantments, and dampens any near it |

A **Masterwork** vessel holds more than its material usually allows, and a master enchanter can
bind two spells into one item.

## Charge

Enchantments on worn items draw a trickle of mana from the wearer. Enchantments on weapons, wands
and tools carry their own **charge**, which runs down with use. Recharge them at an enchanting
bench, at a Spillward Wardhouse for a fee, by leaving them in sunlight wrapped in glow-cloth, or by
feeding them a soul gem, which works fastest and is a crime almost everywhere.

## The accent

Every enchanter leaves a trace in their work: an **accent**, as individual as handwriting. Skilled
wardwrights and Spillward inspectors can read it, tell whose work an enchantment is, and sometimes
tell where and when it was made. Thieves who enchant their own tools learn to hide their accent, or
to borrow someone else's.

In [the Gilded Ascent](../cities/gilded-ascent.md), every enchanted item above Fine must carry a
**maker's mark** registered at the Registry of Seals. An unmarked enchantment is **blank work**,
which is on the Unsealed List.

## Dark enchanting

Dark magic can be bound too, into bone, blood-iron and soul gems, and it does not behave:

- **Hungry items**, such as pact weapons, drink life from what they strike and grow hungry if they
  go unused. A starving weapon feeds on its owner.
- **Cursed items** bind to whoever wears them and cannot be removed without Vitalism or a Spillward
  warden. Some are made cursed on purpose, as gifts.
- **Soul gems** trap a soul to power a great enchantment. The soul is still in there.

| Enchantment | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Thirst** | Part of every hit heals you |
| **Quietus** | Wounds you make cannot be healed |
| **Dreadmark** | Those you strike become terrified |
| **Bloodbound** | The item can't be stolen; it returns to you, through the thief if necessary |
| **Hellforged** | Burns anyone who strikes you with fire that never quite goes out |

Every dark enchantment you make adds to your [corruption](corruption.md), and so, slowly, does
wearing one.
