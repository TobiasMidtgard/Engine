At midnight the Five Quiet Days begin, and the **Great Restriking** with them. In the **Mint of
Seals**, the great die that strikes every seal on the continent has been rebuilt to strike them all
in one night: one seal for every person in every city that signed. When the last is struck, every
one of them becomes a lock, and every lock opens onto the Register, and the Register opens onto the
god.

## Two fronts

> [!WARNING]
> Spoilers for the main story's ending below.

You can't be in two places at once, and the finale is built around that. Your allies take one front;
you take the other, or split them.

### Above: the Mint

The Mint of Seals stands on the Weighhouse tier, and by midnight it is a fortress. Seal Wardens hold
the doors; the Crown Companies of Orravell hold the Toll Rings, if Orravell signed; the Golden
Anchor's airships circle the spire, if Sky City did. Every seal the great die strikes makes Quell
stronger below. Stopping it means getting inside and breaking the die, or seizing it and striking
something else.

How hard this is depends on [The Lantern Table](the-lantern-table.md): with Orath's Wardens, Sky
City's gliders, Keth Veyra's Brinewings and the Tangle's crews beside you, the Mint falls in an
hour. Alone, it is the hardest fight in the game.

### Below: the Register

Gisela Quell waits in the Register room at the bottom of the Counting Deep, with the Sixth Register
open in front of her (or, if you stole it, she has had it brought back by now, and the people who
brought it are your friends in chains). Behind her is the door the Builders never opened. She has
written herself in as the Keeper's hand, and as the great die strikes above, she becomes what she
wrote.

## Gisela Quell

Quell is an Ascendant of **Keeping**: what she keeps can't be taken, and what she locks can't be
opened by anyone not written in her book. She doesn't fight like a warrior. She **locks** things:

- **Doors**: every way out of the room, one by one.
- **You**: one ability at a time, for as long as she holds the lock. Your strongest first.
- **Your companions**: behind doors that weren't there a moment ago.
- **Time**: if she holds Keth Veyra's door, the fight slows around her.
- **Your name**: she tries to write you into the Register, again and again.

What works against her depends on what you have become. A thief of Trespass walks through her
doors. The Unwritten can't be locked, because the lock can't find you. Severance cuts her locks. A
fighter who can't do either breaks the Register's stand while the others hold her. Every seal
struck above heals her; every broken die above weakens her.

She is not a monster, and she says so to the end:

> Nobody drowns in a locked world, nobody sinks, nobody starves for want of a spring they don't own.
> Every door I close is one fewer place to fall. You of all people should understand. You have never
> been kept by anything. Don't you want to be?

## The endings

```flow
start: Quell falls | The Register is open, and the die is stopped or struck.
path: Break every door | Unwrite every well in the Register at once.
path: Give each city its key | Tear out each well's page and give it to the city above it.
path: Keep the keys | Write yourself in as keeper, in Quell's place.
path: Seal the world | Let the die finish; take Quell's place, or let her keep it.
join: Dawn of the first Quiet Day | The world wakes up changed.
end: The epilogue | Every city you touched, as you left it.
```

| Ending | The world after |
|---|---|
| **Break every door** | Every well opens at once, for ever. Sky City climbs above the clouds and its chains snap like threads; the First Spring floods the Hollow Crown; Veyrhold's quarry hums; time runs quick in Keth Veyra's valley. Magic is cheap and dangerous everywhere: spills in every city, the Spillward overwhelmed, wonders and disasters weekly. The God of Locked Doors is weaker than it has been since the Builders. |
| **Give each city its key** | The Register is torn into pages, and each well's page goes to the people who live above it: the Accord of Four Keys, the Council of Three Anchors, the Confluence Compact, the keeper families. Cities that signed tear up their seals, or keep them. Nobody owns every well again, and the next century is an argument between neighbours. |
| **Keep the keys** | Your name goes into the Register where Quell's was. Every well is yours to open or close; every city needs you; every seal is a lock you hold. You are on the road to becoming a god, or to feeding one. The other Ascendants come to see what you have done, and not all of them leave. |
| **Seal the world** | The Restriking completes. Every person in every signing city carries a seal; every lock passes through the Register to the god, who wakes stronger than it has ever been. The continent is orderly, peaceful and locked, and the cities that refused are surrounded. |

## The epilogue

After the ending, the epilogue shows every city you touched, as your choices left it: whether
Orath's Spring flows, whether Sky City floats, whether the Compact holds in the Second City, whether
Kestrel Vale is free, whether Tessara's walls are open mosaics or black bands, whether Ward Null is
on the map. People you helped and people you failed have a line each. Kit Fenn's grave in the
Valley has fresh flowers, or it doesn't.

Then the game goes on. The world is yours to live in, and one door is still shut: the god's. Its
temple lies behind the door at the bottom of the Counting Deep, and the way to it, and to the
question of whether a god can die, is [The Temple of Locked Doors](../the-temple-of-locked-doors.md).

## Rewards

- The ending you chose, and a title to match: **Doorbreaker**, **Keygiver**, **Keyholder** or
  **the Sealer**.
- If Quell lives (some endings let her), a prisoner, an enemy or a very strange friend.
- Whatever is left of the Sixth Register.
