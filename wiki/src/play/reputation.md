Nobody in this world knows who you are by magic. People know what they saw you do, what they were
told, and what they overheard, and they pass it on. A rumour changes as it travels: the guard you
knocked out becomes three guards by the next tavern, and the merchant you saved becomes a prince.

## How people form an opinion

Every person you meet keeps a few **beliefs** about you, each with the evidence behind it:

| Evidence | Weight |
|---|---|
| **Saw it** | Strongest. Hard to argue with. |
| **Was told** by someone they trust | Strong |
| **Overheard** | Medium |
| **A lie** you told them | As strong as they believe it, until it is found out |

Beliefs fade over time unless something renews them. A person's **opinion** of you adds their
beliefs up against their own values: a thief admires a clean burglary that a guard despises; a
priest of the Covenant of Ash respects a healer and distrusts a necromancer. The same deed makes
you friends and enemies at once.

When two people meet, they swap their most interesting beliefs, so news spreads along the paths
people walk: from the docks to the tavern, from the tavern to the market, from the market to the
guardhouse. Kill every witness and a rumour dies with them. Leave one and it reaches the next town
before you do.

## Standing

Opinions roll up into your **standing** with each city and each faction:

| Standing | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Hated** | Guards attack on sight; shops refuse you |
| **Distrusted** | Higher prices, closed doors, guards who follow you |
| **Unknown** | Where you start in most places |
| **Known** | People nod. Shops give fair prices. Locals start here in their home city. |
| **Respected** | Better prices, better work, introductions |
| **Honoured** | Houses open to you, guards look away from small things, people ask for your help by name |
| **Revered** | The city tells stories about you |

Standing with a **guild** is separate, and follows its ranks (see each
[faction](../factions/index.md)): doing the guild's work raises it, crossing the guild lowers it,
and some guilds hate each other's members on principle.

## Fame and infamy

Your **fame** is how much of the continent has heard of you for good things; your **infamy**, for
frightening ones. You can have a great deal of both.

| Fame or infamy | What changes |
|---|---|
| **A little** | People in the next town have heard a story; it might be you |
| **Some** | Strangers know your name, and sometimes your face |
| **A lot** | Guards recognise you; children copy you; disguises are harder |
| **Legendary** | Armies plan around you. Some enemies give up before you arrive; others hire someone to stop you. |

Infamy makes intimidation easier and everything else harder. Fame makes most things easier, except
hiding.

## The world adapts

As your name grows, the world takes you into account. Rob from the roofs and patrols climb onto
them; poison people and the rich hire tasters and alchemical wards; use illusions and the guilds
issue identity tokens that magic can't copy; raise the dead and the cities burn or consecrate their
bodies; break down doors and the doors get thicker. When you are legendary, vaults close their
doors when you enter the city, and soldiers think twice about standing at a checkpoint you are
walking toward.

## Changing what people think

- **Do things**, where people can see them.
- **Pay a crier** or a broadsheet in the Gilded Ascent or Vantress to tell your side.
- **Talk to the person** who started the rumour, and make them take it back (see
  [Conversation](dialogue.md)).
- **Wear a different face**: a disguise, Illusion's Glamour, or a name you bought in Vantress.
- **Earn a title** (see [Titles](titles.md)): a title is a reputation the world has agreed on.

## Relations with people

The people you work with most closely have a relationship with you as well as an opinion:
**companions** approve and disapprove of what you do (see [Companions](companions.md)); guild
leaders remember whose side you took; traders remember whether you paid. Some of them will help you
one day because of something small you did a long time ago.
