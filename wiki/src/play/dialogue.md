Most problems in this world can be talked through, if you know what the other person wants. A
guard wants a quiet shift; a clerk wants not to be blamed; a merchant wants a margin; a vampire
wants to be respected. Conversations are where you find that out, and where your skills, your
reputation and your choices show.

## Checks

Some lines in a conversation are **checks**: a chance, shown before you choose it, that it will
work.

| Check | Skill | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Persuade** | Persuasion | Convince with reasons, sympathy or charm |
| **Deceive** | Deception | Make them believe something false |
| **Intimidate** | Intimidation | Make them afraid of saying no |
| **Haggle** | Trade | Change the price, or the deal |
| **Insight** | Perception | Notice a lie, a fear, a want |
| **Know** | A craft or magic skill | Speak as an expert: a smith about a blade, a mage about a spill |

The chance depends on your **skill**, your **Presence** (or Perception for Insight), the person's
**opinion** of you, what they **believe** about you, and how much you are asking. A guard who has
heard that you cheat people is harder to deceive; one who saw you save a child is easier to
persuade. Asking someone to risk their job is harder than asking them to look away.

### White and red checks

- **White checks** can be tried again after something changes: you learn a new fact, raise the
  skill, bring a gift, or come back another day.
- **Red checks** happen once. Fail and the moment is gone; the conversation goes on from the
  failure, which is often more interesting than success.

## Observations

With good Perception, conversations show you **observations**: small things you noticed. A ring
mark on a finger where there is no ring. Ink on the cuff of a man who says he can't write. A
servant who looks at the stairs every time you mention the cellar. Each one can open a new line of
conversation, and some open lines that no check can.

At low Perception you see few; at the human apex you notice almost everything people don't say.

## Lies

People remember what you told them. A lie that works becomes a **belief** (see
[Reputation](reputation.md)), and if it is ever found out, it turns into a belief that you lie,
which they pass on. A good liar keeps their stories consistent across a city; your journal keeps a
note of what you told whom.

## Reputation and titles in conversation

Your standing, your fame and your [titles](titles.md) add lines of their own: a Respected
customer can ask for credit; someone Honoured in Orath can ask a Keybearer for an audience; a
famous fighter can be challenged, or recognised and avoided. Your [origin](character-creation.md)
gives you lines only a local could say.

## Magic in conversation

Spells cut through conversations, and people know it:

- **Illusion**: Calm settles an angry guard; Truth sense shows you who is lying; Glamour makes you
  someone they like.
- **Domination**: Compel makes someone answer; Fear makes them run; False memory makes them
  remember a conversation you never had.

Most people can tell when they have been magicked, afterwards. Casting on someone in conversation
is a crime in most cities and a grave insult everywhere, so the people you work on remember. The
Night Council of the Second City treats Domination of its members as an act of war.

## Bribes and gifts

Money speaks. A bribe is a check of its own: too little insults, too much makes people suspicious,
and some people can't be bought (Ison can't, and neither can a Tessaran official it can see).
Gifts work better than bribes on people who like you, and on many who don't: a Rillvault grower
values a rare seed more than a purse.

## Dialogue difficulty

Conversations have their own difficulty setting, separate from combat (see
[Difficulty](difficulty.md)): harder makes convincing, deceiving and impressing people harder, and
makes people slower to forget.
