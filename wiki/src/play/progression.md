
{{draft}}

You start as a genuinely ordinary person, slightly below average, who struggles to beat a
[house rat](../creatures/house-rat.md). You are not secretly the chosen one: your health is a
little poor, you have hardly fought, your feel for magic is weak, your gear is cheap, and nobody
knows or protects you. A guard is dangerous. Two guards are terrifying. A locked door is a real
obstacle. By the end you can stand against an army, and even learn how to kill a god.

## Character levels

There are 100 levels, and only three states of being: **Human**, **Awakened** and **Ascendant**.
Most of the game is human; the last two states are earned, never ground out.

| Levels | State | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 1–10 | Ordinary human | From below average to a capable civilian |
| 11–25 | Trained human | A professional: soldier, burglar, hunter, craftsperson, apprentice mage |
| 26–45 | Exceptional human | One of the most capable people in a city |
| 46–60 | Human apex | The very limit of what a real person can do |
| 61–80 | Awakened | Superhuman growth becomes possible |
| 81–100 | Ascendant | You begin to impose your own laws on the world |
| Earned separately | Godslayer | You learn how to kill a god for good |

The three great moments are reaching the human limit, Awakening and Ascending: you master being
human, you break the limits of humanity, and you establish what you are becoming. Then you find
out whether even gods can be made mortal.

## Human: levels 1 to 60

Everything a human apex can do has a believable explanation. A master thief climbs a cathedral
because of perfect technique, the right equipment, knowledge of the route and extraordinary
conditioning, not because they can jump fifteen metres. At level 60 you reach the end of natural
growth: experience can no longer raise your level, and you can see that ceiling coming.

Training improves your mastery of what you are; it never changes what you are. You don't become
a god-killer by doing push-ups.

## Awakening: levels 61 to 80

Breaking the human limit takes a major quest and something genuinely rare. You might survive raw
exposure to a Fundament, bind yourself to a supernatural creature, install an ancient magical or
mechanical core, come back from death with your soul changed, complete a forbidden
transformation, steal an Awakening meant for someone else, be remade by a powerful being, or build
an artificial Awakening through craft and research.

Awakening doesn't hand you power; it lets you grow past human limits: strength beyond muscle,
faster perception, unnatural recovery, direct control of magical energy, resistance to extreme
environments, movement that ignores ordinary limits, a supernatural ability of your own, and a
first touch of souls, ideas and other realms. Level 80 is the known limit of the Awakened.

Everything you mastered as a human still counts: a master thief uses teleportation far more
cleverly than an amateur.

## Ascension: levels 81 to 100

An Awakened person has gone beyond human limits; an Ascendant has gone beyond the limits of the
world. To ascend you must establish something that is yours alone: a **Legend**, a **Domain**, a
**Principle** or a personal law. You might be the first to do something impossible, defeat or
inherit from another Ascendant, invent a new form of magic, claim an idea that nobody owns, go
somewhere outside reality and come back unchanged, or permanently change part of the world.

What you claim grows from how you play. A thief might steal a god's name, a person's fate, the
distance between two places or a god's right to enter its own temple. After 81, levels deepen your
authority rather than your numbers. An **Ascendant of Trespass**, for example, first walks into
sealed places, then through magical barriers, past every rule and guard that would recognise them,
into dreams and memories, between realms, brings friends along, and in the end trespasses into
ideas themselves.

> Does this door still have the authority to keep me out?

Every higher state asks four things of you: the **capacity** (you have mastered your current
state), a **catalyst** (something rare that makes the change possible), a **deed** (proof you can
survive what comes next) and a **price** (a lasting cost, weakness or obligation). Failing a trial
rarely ends the game; it leaves scars, mutations, debts and darker paths instead.

## Godslayer

Godslayer is not a level. It is a qualification you earn by discovering how gods can be destroyed
for good: understanding what a god is, finding the condition that keeps it immortal, making or
finding something that can wound divine essence, stopping it from escaping or being reborn,
surviving its direct power, and living with the consequences. A level 88 Ascendant could be a
Godslayer; a level 100 one might not be.

Every god has its own condition for immortality: its true name is still spoken, its temple still
stands, its death has been erased from history, or it can only be killed by someone it made. The
**God of Locked Doors** cannot be harmed while any door in its temple stays closed, so you must
open every one of them, physical, magical, symbolic and the ones in your own mind, and keep them
open for the whole fight.

## What each level gives

- **Every level**: a small rise in your attributes, one practical improvement you notice at once
  (carrying more, climbing faster, steadier spells, a new observation in conversation), and
  progress toward your next breakthrough.
- **Every few levels**: a meaningful new technique: a dodge that becomes a slide, fire you can shape
  into a line, a burst, a trap or a jet, disguises that carry the access of the person you are
  dressed as.
- **Breakthroughs** are events you prove through play: rob a guarded building without being
  identified, beat a veteran, craft something rare, turn two rival groups against each other.
  How you do it shapes what you get. Time slows, your old limits appear crossed out, and the
  screen shows exactly what changed, while the people around you react.
- **Titles** carry real powers. An **Unseen Guest**, who has slipped in and out unseen many times,
  finds guards slower to confirm a sighting and servants assuming they belong.

## Experience

You earn experience by doing things that matter: discoveries, danger, difficult plans, important
opponents, novel solutions and the consequences you survive. Doing the same easy thing again earns
less and less. Picking simple locks makes you faster; a unique living lock teaches you something
new; robbing a master locksmith can be a breakthrough.

As you grow, the world adapts. Run the roofs and the guards start patrolling them; poison people
and the rich hire tasters; rely on brute force and the doors get thicker. Go back to places you
know and feel how far you've come: the wall that once needed a rope, a distraction and a ladder
becomes a single leap.

## Skill mastery

Alongside your level, every skill improves by using it: fight to fight better, cast to cast
better, sneak to sneak better. Each skill rises to 100 and can then **ascend**, past what any
mortal can do. Ascended skills keep climbing, and the change is visible: your blows crack stone
and fell trees, your spells grow from sparks to suns.

| Rank | Where it comes |
|---|---|
| Novice | Your first steps in a skill |
| Apprentice | |
| Adept | Element pairs open in magic |
| Expert | |
| Master | All three elements combine; the strongest spells |
| Ascended | Past skill level 100 |
| God | Every skill ascended |

## Reflexes and speed

Your level also makes you faster and sharpens your reflexes, so attacks seem to come at you
more and more slowly (see [Movement](movement.md#focus-slowing-time)).
