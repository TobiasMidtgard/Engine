Harder difficulties in this world make enemies **cleverer**, not tougher. An archer on Easy walks
up to the rock you are hiding behind; on Hard it takes its own cover and waits for you to peek; on
Very hard it calls a fighter to flush you out and holds its arrow on the side you will run to.

## The five difficulties

| Difficulty | How enemies fight |
|---|---|
| **Easy** | They come straight at you, one or two at a time, and give up quickly. Animals flee early. |
| **Medium** | They circle, take turns, keep their distance with bows, and shoot at the edge of your cover. |
| **Hard** | They take cover, hold drawn arrows for you, feint and punish your mistakes, guard their necks, and lob arrows over walls if they are good enough. Animals hide in grass and woods, and herds protect their young. |
| **Very hard** | They work as a team: one holds you, one flanks; archers volley together; tanks push your cover while others wait for you to break. |
| **Extreme** | They learn your habits and counter them, bait you with a partner, and go for your healer or your companion first. Wolves surround you and cut off your escape. |

The same ladder holds for every kind of enemy: fighters, archers, gunners, tanks, healers, mages
and assassins. Within a difficulty, a veteran still fights better than a recruit.

Some things never change, whatever the setting:

- Nobody reacts faster than a person can.
- The first shot at you, when they first see you, misses.
- Grenades, lobs and calls for help are shouted, so you can hear them coming.
- Nobody shoots through a wall at where you really are, only where they last saw or heard you.

You can set difficulty for the whole world, or for one kind of enemy: an easier Orravell army with
harder assassins, if that's what you want.

## Dialogue difficulty

Conversations have their own setting. Harder makes it harder to convince, deceive, scam and impress
people, and makes them slower to forget what they think of you (see
[Conversation](dialogue.md)).

## Going down

When your health reaches zero you are **down**. What happens next depends on who put you there:

| Who | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Guards** | You wake in a cell (see [Crime](crime.md#getting-caught)) |
| **Bandits and thieves** | You wake by the road, robbed of your purse and something you liked, with an injury |
| **Arena fighters, duellists** | You lose the bout, and your pride |
| **Monsters, beasts, the undead** | You die |
| **Assassins, the Last Writ, demons** | You die, unless someone paid for you to live |

On **Easy** and **Medium**, people and animals that don't want to kill you, don't: you wake injured
and poorer. On **Hard** and above, anyone in a real fight can kill you. Death sends you back to
your last save.

### Defeat has consequences

Being beaten is part of the story, and the world remembers it. A captured hero may have to break
out, a robbed one may have to find the robbers, and a defeat at the hands of a rival makes them
bolder next time. Failing an [Awakening or Ascension](ascension.md) rarely ends the game: it
leaves scars, a debt, a flaw or a darker road.

## Saving

You can save anywhere outside a fight. The game also saves when you sleep, enter a city, and start
or finish a quest. On **Extreme** you can choose **one life**: one save that the game keeps for you,
and no going back.
